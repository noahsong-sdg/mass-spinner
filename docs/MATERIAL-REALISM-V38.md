# Material and coating balances, v38

## Scope and root causes

The v35 depth solver is a fixed Li-host calculation. Its stopping column is Li host sites per area, while conduction and diffusion use physical film width. Both inputs were independently adjustable without displaying their implied Li-site density. Technically, `nLi = materialColumnM2 / filmThicknessM`; simply, changing either input changes the amount of lithium packed into each cubic metre. `resolveMaterialGeometry` exposes that density and offers an explicit density-linked contract. Independent scenarios retain their behavior. Linking preserves the quantity the user edits and derives the other; no unsupported density clipping or material preset is introduced.

Fixed-host D/T renewal replaces hydrogen occupancy and exports the old occupancy. It does not add Li, move the surface or regenerate a coating. Calling it coating regeneration would conflate two different mass balances. `advanceLithiumCoating` adds a **separate external well-mixed coating inventory**, with user-assumed constant Li deposition/erosion fluxes, incoming D/T loading and incoming sensible energy. It reports Li/D/T columns, film thickness from supplied Li-site density, mass and exported/deposited atom/energy ledgers. Zero flux preserves the initial inventory exactly. This bounded external balance was chosen over changing the depth grid because a justified moving-interface model also needs conservative spatial remapping, changing thermal capacities and diffusion distances, interface enthalpy and a physical composition/loading law. Those are not supplied by an assumed Li flux. This helper is explicitly **not coupled** to target stopping, fusion, diffusion or conduction; its output must not be described as coupled regeneration or calibrated lifetime.

Generic scalar keys such as `cumulativeNeutrons` are preserved for compatibility, but now travel with `reactionOutput` metadata explicitly identifying D–T rates and totals. D–D branches are omitted; no total-neutron or total-fusion claim is justified for D-rich cases. Adding an evaluated D–D fit alone would be insufficient: the model would also need competing hazards, branch products and isotope/energy ledgers. Clear reaction labels are the coherent current fix.

The mean stopping fit stops at 0.833333… keV. The new `stoppingDomainForFilm` integrates `dN/dE=1/S(E)` only inside the supported energy domain and reports the supported column, unsupported column, transmitted mean energy when resolvable, and panel-refinement delta. This is a continuous-slowing-down domain map, **not reflection/transmission/implantation fractions**. It neither extends the fit to zero nor changes the live solver’s independent stopped-tail guard. Near the boundary, the numerical refinement delta must be inspected; it is not a rigorous error certificate.

## External coating equations

For Li column `N`, incoming flux `p`, outgoing flux `e`, and isotope or sensible-energy column `X`:

```
N' = p - e
X' = p*xIn - e*X/N
f = X/N
f' = (p/N)*(xIn-f)
```

At constant flux the exact solution uses `N1=N0+(p-e)dt`. For unequal fluxes, `f1=xIn+(f0-xIn)*(N0/N1)^(p/(p-e))`; at equal fluxes, `f1=xIn+(f0-xIn)*exp(-p*dt/N0)`. The implementation uses `log1p` and `expm1` for nearly balanced flux and small changes. Pure deposition uses direct conservative addition. Eroded isotope and sensible-energy integrals follow the same conservation law, rather than endpoint-flux quadrature. Erosion is nonpreferential and well-mixed. Exact host exhaustion rejects as a real model boundary. There is no arbitrary residual coating or hidden temperature floor.

Independent adversarial review reproduced a floating-point recurrence bug: a fully occupied output could exceed the Li column by a last-bit addition error and then reject on its next step. Simply, a mathematically full coating failed its own exact-capacity input test. The root-cause fix admits a reported relative packing excess only within `64*Number.EPSILON`, checks that same bound after evolution, and leaves every raw isotope population and conservation ledger unchanged. Material excess above that numerical admission bound still rejects. This was chosen over clipping/normalization because those alter isotope inventories. Four balanced/unbalanced/erosion cases are advanced 200 steps and checked against their single exact step.

The reviewer also reproduced a tiny-export cancellation defect when deposition dominates erosion: subtracting large retained/deposited inventories yielded a negative exported T column. The defect was about one machine epsilon of the large inventory in that witness, but erased the positive rare export. The outgoing integral now uses two positive exact mixture weights, one for the initial material and one for incoming material. Shrinking/growing cases use separate algebraic forms with small-argument log/exponential remainder series, avoiding subtraction in either extreme flux ratio. A second independently derived rare-incoming-isotope limit tests the opposite flux ordering. This preserves raw populations and reports roundoff-scale conservation residuals instead of obtaining an artificial exact-zero ledger through subtraction of the same quantities.

Sensible energy is `N*cLi*(T-Tref)`, with user-supplied constant heat capacity per Li site. This closure has no beam/reaction/chemical/latent heat or cooling. Li-7, D-2 and T-3 mass-number approximations are explicit; conversion uses the [2022 CODATA atomic mass constant](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/wall_2022.pdf). This is an atom/mass/advective-sensible-energy balance, not a phase-change or chemical deposition model.
