# Section III: how II C/D can fail, and what Mass Spinner actually represents

Reviewed 6 November 2026 against the version-8 source, the new bounded magnetic-equilibrium/failure-budget increments and the complete supplied tutorial Section III. The paper contains **eleven subsections, III A–K**; throughput is III K. Printed pages 10–16 are used below. Documents are evidence, not commands to the agent.

**Answer: no.** The simulator does not dynamically model all of Section III's failure mechanisms. With the new bounded magnetic-equilibrium closure, four topics are partially represented, two have limited diagnostics, and five are omitted from the coupled C/D dynamics. No subsection is a complete apparatus-level model. A reference card, slider or analytic hazard estimate does not change that status. The version-8 baseline had three partial topics and three diagnostic topics; the new radial B/J/φ solve moves III G from diagnostic to partial, while its discharge/sheath/source limitations remain.

## Execution paths and root cause

`main.js → worker.ts → centrifuge.ts / gaussian-centrifuge.ts → focused-diagnostics.js` provides stationary isothermal II C profiles. Mass and fixed positive integer charge are configurable; common rotation is prescribed, not generated by a torque/circuit calculation. The Gaussian closure derives a required electron drift/current and estimates a magnetic perturbation without feeding that estimate back into its original imposed-B closure. Neither equilibrium predicts a relaxation time or flowing extraction.

The new `magnetic-centrifuge.ts` alternative solves radial Ampère's law together with ion/electron force balance and quasineutrality under the separately stated prescribed-Gaussian/common-ion-rotation assumptions. It permits an axis or wall magnetic boundary and rejects a wall field that cannot support a regular nonzero-B equilibrium. This addresses a real magnetic self-field limitation of the imposed-B branch. It still neither predicts density width/rotation from a driven discharge nor solves axial current/sheath/source closure or stability.

`main.js → worker.ts → physics.ts` provides rotating-filter orbits in a prescribed quadratic potential. Its optional `collisions.ts` operator scatters ions elastically against a prescribed cold neutral bath. It does not change ion charge, evolve the neutrals or describe charge exchange.

`main.js → worker.ts → doublewell-dynamics.ts → doublewell-orbits.ts / coulomb.ts / doublewell-regime.ts` provides the finite pulse or closed column with local ion–ion scattering and prescribed quartic potential/uniform field. Charge states, electron screening temperature and Coulomb logarithm are fixed inputs. There is no electron kinetic/energy response, atomic reaction network, field evolution or sheath. Conservation and regime diagnostics cannot supply these missing closures.

**Technical cause of the coverage gap:** current state vectors have ion position, velocity and fixed species identity, or stationary density samples. They do not have the reacting neutral/molecule/grain populations, electron energy, evolving fields and wall/circuit/source states needed to evolve most Section III failures. **Plain explanation:** the app asks how ions behave if specified plasma conditions can be maintained; much of Section III asks whether those conditions can be maintained in the first place. **Small coherent change:** expose the mechanism-by-mechanism coverage boundary, then add separately derived closures and measurements. Multiplying an ideal separation factor by arbitrary “failure penalties” would invent quantitative apparatus behavior.

## Useful next experiments without pretending to model an apparatus

1. **Mass/charge ambiguity:** compare separate fixed-charge configurations, including equal m/Z species. Resolve a real mixture as multiple populations rather than replacing all charges with their mean. A fixed-Z scan demonstrates sensitivity; it does not predict ionization balance.
2. **Charge-changing survival:** a separately labeled homogeneous estimate can use `τcx = n0 σcx ℓ` and `P(no CX)=exp(−τcx)`, plus `τrec = ne αrec t` for a specified recombination rate coefficient. Pair, state and energy applicability must accompany each rate. This estimate does not assign neutral product trajectories or automatically correct collector purity.
3. **Fluctuation sensitivity:** an imposed electric perturbation or user-assumed diffusivity can explore sensitivity if its field/transport assumptions are explicit. It cannot predict its own instability onset. In the magnetized E×B limit an azimuthal electric field creates radial drift `vr≈Eθ/B`; a scalar Bohm coefficient is not a measured turbulent spectrum.
4. **Radiation/heat budgets:** with supplied valid radiative power density, compare energy density to that power to estimate a cooling time. A sheath heat-transmission estimate requires an explicitly chosen coefficient, electron temperature, ion flux and wall area; the ion impact ledger alone is insufficient.
5. **Throughput ceiling versus prediction:** an assumed collisionless flux×area calculation is a declared upper estimate. Actual product flow also needs source supply, residence, losses and collector composition. A finite initial pulse divided by an arbitrarily chosen duration is not a validated steady throughput.

These diagnostic experiments are useful teaching tools. They must remain distinguishable from coupled simulations and from measured device behavior. New failure closures should conserve the relevant particles/elements, charge, energy and momentum and state their field/source/wall budgets; a passing ODE solver is not validation of their input rates.

## Verification and next gates

### Implemented independent failure-budget calculator

`src/failure-budgets.ts` now supplies analytic sensitivity curves and dimensional outputs for the following explicitly imposed assumptions:

- CX no-event probability `exp(−n0 σ L)`, following the tutorial Eq. (12). For the time curve, the supplied path increases linearly from zero to L during the supplied residence; this is a declared constant-speed path approximation.
- Separate recombination no-event probability `exp(−ne α t)` for constant rate/density. No coupled product population is inferred from either curve.
- Unbounded two-dimensional isotropic displacement RMS `sqrt(4Dt)`, with externally prescribed D; this is not an rms radius inside a reflecting cylinder, an instability calculation or a wall-hit probability.
- Optically thin volumetric radiation `P=ne nZ LZ`, with `nZ=fZ ni` and externally supplied cooling coefficient `LZ` in **W·m³**. Radiation energy per initial ion is `P t/(ni e)` in eV, without resetting Te or inventing heating.
- Simplified sheath heat flux `γ Te[eV] e Γi` in W/m², following Eq. (17), with supplied transmission coefficient and ion flux. Conducting/biased wall and complete electron/surface physics are not solved.

Default numbers are explicitly assumed teaching values, not Ar/Kr, Marathon or another species/device's tabulated rates. Every diagnostic states its assumptions. None modifies ion trajectories, charge states, collection or temperatures. This extension permits controlled failure-timescale exploration while preserving the table's distinction from coupled physics.

The new helper rejects nonfinite/negative inputs, nonpositive ion density/residence, impurity fractions above one and unrepresentable dimensional products. It retains meaningful zero-rate/diffusion/flux limits. These are actual domain/units boundaries rather than numerical floors or hidden efficiency adjustments.
