Centrifuge and reactors

These three systems do different work. The centrifuge separates isotopes. The beam reactor supplies neutrons. A power reactor would supply heat for electricity production.

  2. Beam reactor
  3. Power reactor

Centrifuge

A fusion plant must have fuel with the correct isotope mixture. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different masses.

The centrifuge turns gas to change this mixture. Electrical current across a magnetic field causes a force that turns charged gas around the axis. Collisions cause neutral atoms to turn. These atoms have no electric charge. The vessel and electrodes do not move.

During equilibrium rotation, the number of atoms at each radius does not change. With the same temperature and angular speed at all radii, the light-isotope percentage is larger near the axis. The heavy-isotope percentage is larger near the wall. Collection from different regions can give different mixtures. Separation does not make atoms or cause fusion.

The simulator calculates rotation, isotope percentages at different radii, and optional temperature changes. Its collection calculation divides the atoms in the vessel into regions. It does not calculate extraction flow or show the performance of a Marathon machine.

Beam reactor

A compact neutron source can supply neutrons for material tests. A neutron is a nuclear particle with no electric charge.

Deuterium and tritium are hydrogen isotopes. An ion is an atom with an electric charge.

An electric field increases the energy of deuterium ions. The beam enters a solid lithium tritide target, which contains tritium. The nucleus is the central part of an atom. Fusion changes a deuterium nucleus and a tritium nucleus into a helium nucleus and a fast neutron. The reaction releases energy.

The energy of most beam ions decreases in the target without fusion. They heat the target, so cooling is necessary. Target composition also changes during exposure. These effects change neutron output and target conditions.

The simulator calculates beam slowing, neutron production, and target heat and tritium content under specified assumptions. It does not show net electrical power production. This source is separate from the future power-reactor concept.

Power reactor

A power reactor must supply more electrical power than its equipment uses. A complete energy account is necessary.

This concept uses a hot deuterium–tritium plasma. A plasma contains ions and free electrons. Electrons have a negative electric charge. Magnetic fields help keep the plasma inside the chamber. Fusion neutrons enter a structure around the plasma called the blanket.

The blanket absorbs neutron energy as heat. A coolant would carry heat to equipment that supplies electrical power. Neutron reactions with lithium also supply tritium to replace fuel used in fusion.

Marathon’s proposal also includes mercury reactions for gold production in a power-reactor blanket. This proposal is separate from the compact beam reactor. A centrifuge can prepare enriched lithium or separate used fuel for a fusion plant. It does not supply fusion power.

The site shows a blanket reference and radioactive-decay calculations. Some dimensions come from the published blanket model. Other hardware shows a concept. The site does not calculate a complete power plant or calculate net electricity production.
