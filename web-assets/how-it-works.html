How Mass Spinner works

A Marathon-inspired centrifuge playground. Other models are under Advanced.

Separators move ions and neutral atoms. The separate beam-target model estimates neutrons from D–T fusion. Neutrinos are different particles and are not modeled.

What moves, and why

Advanced → Mass Spinner / Models
Prescribed-field ion orbits, resonant heating, rotating filters and equilibrium models. The orbit integrator advances the Lorentz force:

m dv/dt = q(E + v × B)

Centrifuge
Current drives ions; friction spins up neutral gas. Radial transport changes composition and gas inertia; optional thermal evolution tracks work and heat.

dp/dr = ρΩ²r
ns(r) ∝ exp[msΩ²r²/(2kBT)]

These are equilibrium references; the second assumes ideal, isothermal, rigidly rotating neutral gas. Heavier atoms favor the wall. Faster rotation strengthens separation; heating weakens it. The live model does not solve radial fluid momentum or extraction.

Torque and heat equations

dLi/dt = τem − Γ(Ωi − Ωn)
dLn/dt = Γ(Ωi − Ωn) − τwall

L = IΩ: angular momentum equals inertia × angular speed. Redistribution changes neutral inertia, so conserved momentum determines speed. Γ is the ion–neutral coupling coefficient.

Ugas = 3NkBT/2   ·   qheat = −k∇T

Drive work, friction, transported enthalpy and cooling have separate energy ledgers. Numerical damping is kept separate from physical heat.

SI units: mass m in kg; radius r in m; temperature T in K; angular speed Ω in rad/s; torque τ in N·m; angular momentum L in kg·m²/s; heat flux qheat in W/m². kB is Boltzmann’s constant; k is thermal conductivity.
Advanced → Marathon systems
Deuteron stopping, reaction cross sections, target loading and heat determine neutron output. The future blanket is a separate conceptual calculation, not a full reactor simulation.
Neutron-source equation

pfusion = 1 − exp(−∫nTσ[Ecm(x)] dx)
Rn = (Ibeam/e)pfusion

Target tritium density and the slowing deuteron's reaction probability set neutron rate. Fusion output and accelerator input are separate ledgers; this is not a net-electricity prediction.

What the computer solves

Live: settings → TypeScript worker → computed history → plots and Three.js scene. Animated particles represent populations; the render is not a resolved 3D plasma.

Ion orbits use Boris integration. The live centrifuge uses radial finite volumes, Scharfetter–Gummel transport fluxes and implicit backward-Euler steps. Conductivity, coupling and diffusion inputs remain prescribed.

Offline: Python / NumPy / SciPy / FiPy evolve an axisymmetric gas mixture: density, radial and axial momentum, angular momentum, total energy and species mass. Compressible transport is split from current, shear and thermal sources.

That research solver is different from the browser engine. The Research playground replays saved collision studies; it does not run the new gas-flow solver.

What accuracy requires

  1. Verify the numerics. Conservation, exact-solution controls and mesh/timestep refinement test whether equations are implemented and resolved correctly.
  2. Validate against experiments. Match apparatus, inputs and measurement method. Bound uncertainty, calibrate declared nuisance inputs, then predict held-out conditions without refitting.
  3. Qualify the live playground. Establish those limits for the actual browser model and parameter range, then verify sliders, plots and 3D views use the same result. This is our project label, not a certification.

First-order hardware fidelity is blocked mainly by experimental constraints, uncertain plasma/wall closures and untested regimes. The audited microkelvin wall-response discrepancy is secondary to those, though relative heating remains unqualified.

A numerical pass is not experimental accuracy. The live rigid-rotation family fails the retrospective argon profile screen under the stated optical interpretation. The lithium playground remains pedagogical and experimentally unqualified.

What this could be useful for

Now: learning, scientific visualization and a portfolio of reproducible modeling. After bounded validation: sensitivity studies, operating-point screening, experiment planning and thermal/fuel-cycle estimates. Hardware design, safety or performance guarantees need separate evidence.

Relevant work includes scientific software, computational plasma physics, thermal/fluids modeling, experimental diagnostics and verification. A useful deliverable is a defensible prediction with its uncertainty.
